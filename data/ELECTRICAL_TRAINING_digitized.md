WHAT IS ,, VOLTAGE“?

> Voltage is a measure of the electrical force that seems to push the
current along.

>» Created by power sources e.g. Batteries, generators, power plants

> It “transports” current (the flow of electrons) through wires and
devices.

> Higher voltage means a stronger push, allowing more energy to be
delivered.

> Types of current:
> DC (Direct Current): flows in one direction (batteries)
> AC (Alternating Current): changes direction back and forth

> Water analogy:
> Voltage = pressure

> Measured in Volt (V)
> The symbol for voltage is ,,V“

WHAT IS ,,RESISTANCE“?

> Resistance used to measure the opposition to the flow of electrical current.

>» What causes resistance?
> The material (rubber has high resistance, copper has low)
> Length of the conductor (longer wire — more resistance)
> Thickness (thicker wire — lower resistance)
> Temperature (higher temp usually increases resistance)

> Water analogy:
>» Resistance = pipe narrowness
> Anarrow pipe resists water: flow — less flow
>» Awide pipe allows more flow

> Measured in Ohm (Q),
> The symbol for resistance is ,,R“

EVALUATING THE RISKS

> After you recognize a hazard, your next step is to reduce your risk from the hazard.
> The closer you work to the ,danger zone“, the more likely you'll be exposed to the electrical hazards.

> Wet conditions combined with other hazards also increase your risk. You will need to make decisions

about the nature of hazards in order to reduce your risk and do the right thing to remain safe.

> Another factor increasing your risk of injury is working around combinations of hazards.

What work is a non-electrician allowed to perform?
>» Nominal voltage up to 50 VAC or 120 VDC

> Assuming that risks due to arcing are ruled out

> Connection of pluggable equipment

>» Nominal voltage above 50 VAC or 120 VDC
> Only by qualified electrician

What does this mean in practical terms?

> You are not allowed to connect an operation container to the public grid or to a power generator.
> You are not allowed to connect non-pluggable pumps or other consumers.

> You are not allowed to repair defective lines or cables.

> You are not allowed to replace three-phase current or fuses, etc.

WORKING PRINCIPLE / HOW DOES THE
PROGRAM WORK IN A PLC?

1. After the PLC is switched on, the processor (which represents the brain of the PLC) questions if
the individual inputs have been transmitted or not. This status of the input is stored in the

process- image input table (PII). Leading inputs become the information 1 or High when enabled,
or the information 0 or Low when not enabled.

2. This processor processes the program deposited into the program memory. This consists of a
list of logic functions and instructions, which are successively processed, so that the required
input information will already be accessed before the read in Pll and the matching results are
written into a process-image output table (PIQ). Also other storage areas for counters, timers and
memory bits will be accessed during program processing by the processor if necessary.

3. Inthe third step after the processing of the user program, the status from the PIQ will transfer to
the outputs and then be switched on and/or off. Afterwards it continues to operate, as seen in
point 1.
